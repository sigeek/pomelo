The problem

is all most owners type. The answer sounds like a chain, so they decide AI isn't for them.

0 new skills

needed. Every owner already knows how to onboard a new employee. That's the whole trick.

How it works

  1. Mira (fictional) owns Kiez Café. A 1-star review: "Waited 20 minutes for one coffee. Never again." She asks her AI intern for a reply.
  2. The first try is a stock apology. She rates it 2 stars: too generic.
  3. Her AI intern asks what a new barista would: what happened, how she sounds, what the guest should do. She taps three answers.
  4. The new reply sounds like her, and every answer lights up in its own colour: a Saturday rush, one barista sick1 signed, Mira2 your next coffee is on me3
  5. She keeps a reusable note for any AI chat. Her first message covered 1 of 4 things a new employee needs; her note covers 4 of 4.

How Claude is used

AI intern

Writes the reply or post, never invents facts, and tags every phrase with the answer it came from.

Coach

Spots which of what, who, tone and goal are missing and asks 3 plain questions, with tappable answers.

Lesson

Turns it all into a reusable note with blanks, what changed, and a one-line takeaway.

Claude Sonnet 5.5 through the Anthropic API, structured output validated on the server, labelled demo data if a call fails, and the owner ticks "I've checked it" before anything is copied. Nothing is stored or posted.
